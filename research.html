2017 — 2020 · Peking University

Superconducting detectors on dSQUID

M.Sc. Condensed matter physics

Master’s work on high-Tc SQUID magnetometers: fabrication, shielding compensation, and a mobile platform that could lock and move in an unshielded environment. Later I joined the making of kinetic-inductance detectors on MgB2 ultrathin films (microwave, optical response, quasiparticle lifetime).

What a SQUID is

A Superconducting Quantum Interference Device is two Josephson junctions on a superconducting loop. The loop’s interference is extremely sensitive to magnetic flux—useful as a magnetometer, and, in related circuits, as a building block for detectors.

How we made them

High-Tc films, junction craft, and compensation so the device could live outside a shielded room. The unglamorous part was process yield and the mechanics of a lockable, movable stage. Thesis title, supervisor, and the step that failed: still to be written from the old notes.

What they are for

Magnetometry in noisy environments; a cousin of the kinetic-inductance detectors used in astronomy. I once presented on superconducting KIDs and single-photon detection. That is the honest bridge if I return to instruments rather than to condensed-matter as a career.
