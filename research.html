2017.10 — 2020.07 · Peking University

HTS rf SQUID magnetometer fabrication and mobile tests

HTS SQUID Magnetometer

Topic

A SQUID (superconducting quantum interference device) is a superconducting loop with Josephson junctions. It is extremely sensitive to magnetic flux, which is why it makes a magnetometer. High-Tc (HTS) devices can run at liquid-nitrogen temperature. The practical problem in the lab was not only to make samples, but to make them work outside a perfect shield—in a simple shielding room, and then on a platform that could move.

Work

  1. Worked through fabrication and shielding-compensation issues to develop HTS rf SQUID samples with consistent performance indices.
  2. Established and tested an environmental magnetic-field compensation system using HTS SQUID magnetometers as background-field detectors, which improved the working environment in the simple magnetic shielding room at Peking University.
  3. Developed an HTS SQUID mobile platform that can lock and move in an unshielded environment. Operational characteristics of the magnetometer were measured under different noise backgrounds and attitude changes.

2018.10 — 2019.07 · Peking University

Lumped-element kinetic inductance detectors based on MgB2 thin films

MgB2 LEKID X-ray detection

Topic

Kinetic inductance detectors read a superconducting film through its inductance, not through a junction. Lumped-element KIDs (LEKIDs) on magnesium diboride (MgB2) thin films were a parallel project in the same period, aimed at whether this material stack could be useful for X-ray detection.

Work

  1. Used microwave testing to observe the optical response of MgB2 lumped-element kinetic inductance detectors. The results were used to explore the application prospect of MgB2 LEKIDs in X-ray detection.
  2. Used a direct-current method to measure the quasiparticle lifetime of the superconducting detector.

2014.10 — 2016.11 · Wuhan University of Technology

Magnetic and transport properties of two-dimensional nanomaterials, first-principles computation

DFT Silicene Phosphorene

Topic

Undergraduate work was first-principles calculation, not a growth lab. The objects were two-dimensional materials: zigzag silicene nanoribbons, and gold-doped phosphorene. The questions were magnetic and transport properties—half-metallicity, spin filtering, and optical response—computed with density-functional theory.

Work

  1. Investigated chemical doping of zigzag silicene nanoribbons (ZSiNRs), with Mn as the passivating element replacing hydrogen at one edge, by first-principles calculations.
  2. Discussed and explained the half-metallic properties of doped ZSiNRs using spin-polarization theory.
  3. Explained the spin-filtering property of doped ZSiNRs from the transmission spectrum and molecular-orbital theory, as a possible path to spintronic devices.
  4. Studied the electronic and optical properties of single- and double-Au-doped phosphorene using a first-principles plane-wave pseudopotential method based on density-functional theory.
